01 / The ideaLess gear. More understanding.

A simple rig.
A considered design.

Plug in a guitar. Shape its character. Hear it through a speaker. MultiFX brings that familiar experience into one compact engineering project.

The goal is a standalone practice amplifier with clean and driven sounds, equalization, and controls you can reach for. Every interface is part of the work, from the pickups to the power stage.

Mono practice amp

02 / Inside the signal

Five stages. One continuous audio path.
Select a stage to explore the proposed architecture.

01 / Input conditioner

Respect the guitar.

A high-impedance input should preserve the character of the pickups while adapting the signal level for the DSP module. Gain, bias, noise, and headroom need to work together.

External circuit · design and component selection pending

02 / Analog-to-digital conversion

A signal becomes samples.

The conditioned guitar signal enters an ADC on the ADAU1701. This is where the analog path becomes digital; its usable range must be checked against the actual input circuit.

Inside the TSA1702B module · input levels to verify

03 / ADAU1701 processing

Give the sound a direction.

A SigmaStudio program is proposed for clean routing, drive, equalization, and level management. The module's potentiometers will adjust parameters; their final assignments are still open.

Dedicated audio DSP · program and behavior to validate

04 / Digital-to-analog conversion

Back into the physical world.

The DAC reconstructs an analog output from the processed samples. This signal feeds the power amplifier, with gain staging chosen to avoid accidental clipping.

Inside the TSA1702B module · output compatibility to verify

05 / Power amplifier and speaker

Make the signal heard.

A separate commercial amplifier supplies the power that drives the speaker. Their selection will determine the supply needs, usable output, and acoustic character of the prototype.

External modules · amplifier, speaker, and ratings not yet selected
↳

Control takes its own path.

A proposed STM32 controller reads buttons, drives LEDs, and changes DSP parameters over I²C. The audio stays in the DSP module. External control access and startup coordination still need verification.

MCU → I²C → DSP

02

Bench validation

Next

Identify the actual hardware, demonstrate clean audio, verify autonomous DSP startup, and measure a parameter change commanded by an external controller. Results will be recorded with their test conditions.

03

PCB & integration

Planned

Bring control, power distribution, and module connections onto a custom main PCB. The board must use the professor's permitted Altium components; other obtainable components connect through headers.

04

Complete prototype

Planned

Integrate the enclosure, controls, amplification, and speaker. Then evaluate the complete system: sound, continuity, electrical limits, power demand, and thermal behavior.

Current evidence: architecture documentation. Complete-system audio, power, and thermal performance have not yet been validated.
